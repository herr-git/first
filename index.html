What it is
A private demo that collects prices for crypto tokens and crypto funds from free public sources and shows them in one place.
What it is not
Not a real trading app and not investment advice. There is no login and no real money. The paper trading part uses pretend money, and no order ever leaves this site.
Where the data comes from
CoinGecko (crypto tokens and tokenized funds) and Yahoo Finance (exchange-traded funds in the US and Europe). An automatic job collects new numbers regularly. Each table says exactly when it was last updated.
What is sample data
Sample data is always marked with a yellow "Sample data" label. If you see no label, the numbers are real, from the sources above.

Exchange-traded crypto funds (US and Europe)

Exchange-traded crypto funds hold crypto and are bought and sold on a normal stock exchange, like a share. In Europe most of them are legally "exchange-traded notes" (a kind of bond backed by the crypto), but for a buyer they work much like a fund.

  • Prices are in the currency each fund trades in: US dollars ($), Swiss francs (CHF) or euros (EUR). They are not converted. Sorting by price or market value keeps each currency together.
  • Market value is the fund's total size as reported by Yahoo, where it has it. It can be a few days behind the price.
  • Volume today is the number of fund shares (or units, in Europe) that changed hands today.
  • Bid and ask are shown only when they are within 1% of the price. Otherwise they say "not available".
  • Funds listed in one country may not be available to buy in another.
  • Paper trading covers the US-listed funds only. "Pretend trade" opens the paper trading page. Paper trading. Pretend money. No real orders.

Tokenized funds

Tokenized funds are ordinary investment funds whose shares are issued as tokens on a blockchain. Free data for them is limited: they are mostly bought directly from the issuer, so they show little or no trading and no bid/ask.

  • Bid and ask: not available. These funds are bought and sold directly with the issuer, and no free source publishes these prices.
  • A volume of $0 is what the source reports: little or no trading on exchanges in the last 24 hours.
  • "What it tracks" is a short summary written for this demo. The issuer's own page has the full details.
  • Tokenized funds can be viewed here but not used for paper trading.

What do these words mean?
Price
The cost of one token, fund share or product unit.
Market value
For a token: price times the number of tokens in circulation. For a fund: the total value of everything the fund holds.
24-hour volume
How much was traded in the last 24 hours. Crypto trades all day, every day.
Volume today
How many fund shares or product units changed hands today on the stock exchange.
Stablecoin
A token designed to stay at a fixed price, usually 1 US dollar.
Bid / ask
Bid is the highest price a buyer is offering right now. Ask is the lowest price a seller will accept.
Yearly fee
What the fund charges each year, as a percentage of the money invested.
Delayed 15 minutes
Free stock market data shows prices from about 15 minutes ago, not this second.
Ticker
The short code a fund or product trades under, for example IBIT.
Tokenized fund
A normal investment fund whose shares exist as tokens on a blockchain, so they can be held in a crypto wallet.
Exchange-traded product
Something that tracks an asset (here, a crypto coin) and is bought and sold on a normal stock exchange, like a share.
Treasury bills
Short-term loans to the US government. They are seen as very low risk.
Staking rewards
A payment some crypto networks make to holders who help run the network.
Sample data
Made-up numbers used to show how the page works. They are not real prices and always carry a yellow "Sample data" label.
